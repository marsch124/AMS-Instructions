How This Works

What this app is for

A quick-reference guide system built around 3-digit numbers. Attach labelled cards with numbers (001–999) to the places that matter — the bed, the steering wheel, the water filler, the gas locker — then scan or type the number and the instruction for that exact job appears.

The point is that you don't have to remember anything at the moment you need it. The knowledge lives on the number, not in your head, and not in a folder in a drawer. It also means somebody else can do the job properly the first time: hand them the number, and they get your steps, your warnings and your photos.

For example: a card taped inside the gas locker reads 118. Anyone opening that locker can scan it and get "Changing the Gas Bottle" — the warning about the flame, the order the valves come off in, and a photo of the regulator seated correctly.

Getting around

Four tabs along the bottom of the screen, always one tap away — and each one has its own colour. The whole screen takes that colour, so you can tell where you are out of the corner of your eye, before you have read a single word:

  • Home — pink. The big Scan button, your Favourites and Recently Viewed, plus any nudge cards (something due, to-dos waiting, a run half-finished, backup overdue).
  • Instructions — green. Every instruction you have, browsable by category, searchable, and filterable. Tap ＋ in the top right to write a new one.
  • Actions — red. Your to-do list. A red number on the tab tells you how many are still open, so you can see it without going there.
  • Settings — blue. The housekeeping: Run a Set and Library Health, People, backups and Data Safety, this guide, and the Version Log.

Anything you open from a tab — an instruction, an editor, the People list — hides the tabs and gives you a ← Back button instead, so you always know whether you are on a main screen or inside something. It keeps the colour of the tab you came from, so a green instruction screen is one you reached from Instructions and Back will take you there.

Two colours mean the same thing everywhere, whatever tab you are on: a filled button is the thing you came to do (Save, Back Up Now, Done), and an outlined red button is a destructive one (Delete, Clear All Data). If a button is outlined in red, read it twice.

Setting it up

  1. Start with the library, or start empty. Settings → Restore from a Backup File will load the 205-instruction starter library if you have the file — RV, home, diving, running, cycling, swimming, golf, meditation, mobility and strength. Every one of them is an ordinary instruction you can edit, rewrite or delete. Or ignore it entirely and write your own from scratch.
  2. Write an instruction. Instructions tab → ＋ in the top right. Give it a 3-digit number, a title, and the steps — one per line. Everything else (warnings, equipment, preparations, frequency, owner, photos) is optional and can be filled in later.
  3. Print the numbers. Make cards or labels with the 3-digit numbers on them. Plain printed digits are enough — you can always type the number. If you want to scan, a free QR generator (qr-server.com) will turn the number into a code.
  4. Attach them where the job happens. Duro tape or adhesive, on or beside the thing itself: the water filler, the gas locker, the awning arm, the bed mechanism.
  5. Add the people. Settings → Manage People. Anna and Martin are there already; add anyone else who does jobs. Now you can say whose instruction each one is.
  6. Back it up. Settings → 💾 Back Up Now, then Save to Files or iCloud Drive. Do this the day you finish setting up, not the day you need it.

Finding an instruction

Browsing by category

The Instructions tab is grouped into folding sections — one per category, each with a coloured dot and a count. Tap a header to open it, tap again to close. It remembers what you left open, and "Expand all" / "Collapse all" beside the count line does the lot at once. This is the way to find something when you do not know what to search for.

Search

Type in the search box and the folders drop away, giving you one plain list of every match across all categories, with the category shown on each result. Searching by number works too. The box clears itself each time you come back to the tab, so you never land on a filtered list wondering where everything went.

Search reads the whole instruction, not just the title

Steps, description, warnings, equipment, preparations, after-use, maintenance, notes, tags, location and owner are all searched. Where the match is somewhere you cannot see from the list, the result says so underneath — "matched in Steps" — so a hit on hidden text never looks like a mistake.

For example: you once typed the tyre pressure into step 4 of the pre-drive check and have long forgotten which instruction it was in. Search 3.5 and it comes back, marked "matched in Steps".

Filters — narrowing the list rather than searching it

Search answers "where did I write that?". Filters answer "what needs my attention?". Tap Filter beside the search box and a panel folds open with five groups of chips. Every chip carries a count, so you can see how much of each thing there is before you tap anything — and a chip nothing matches is greyed out rather than hidden, because "0 overdue" is worth knowing too.

  • Whose job it is — one chip per person, plus Nobody named. This reads the Owner field, and it understands both an owner picked from the People list and one that came in as a plain name from an import, so nobody's work goes missing.
  • When it's due — Overdue, Due within a week, Never done (it has a frequency but has never been marked Done, so its clock has never started), and No repeat (nothing that falls due on a date — "Before each trip", "As-needed", or no frequency at all).
  • Status — Active, Draft, Review Needed, Archived.
  • Gaps worth a second look — No warning, No photo, Barely any steps (fewer than two). None of these is an error; they are the half-finished corners of the library.
  • Quick marks — Favourite, Has a photo, Has an open to-do, Never audited.

How chips combine: chips in the same group widen the net, chips in different groups narrow it. Anna + Martin gives you everything either of them owns. Anna + Overdue gives you only Anna's overdue work.

The moment any filter is on, the folders drop away and you get one plain list, exactly as searching does. The count line reads "12 of 205" so you always know you are looking at a slice, the Filter button turns green and shows how many chips are on, and Clear filters sits right there beside the count. You can search inside a filtered list — the search box and the filters apply together.

Filters are forgotten every time you leave the tab, exactly like the search box. A filter you set yesterday and forgot about would show you a short list today and look for all the world like missing data.

For example: tap 👤 Anna and ⏰ Overdue before a Sunday morning conversation, and you have her list — nothing else. Or tap ⚠️ No warning on a rainy afternoon and work down the instructions that still need their real hazard written in.

Doing something to the whole filtered list

Once a filter is on, a dashed ⚡ Apply to all 12… button appears under the count. It opens three actions that work on exactly the rows on screen — no more, no less:

  • Set owner — the one that turns a filter into a day's work saved. Filter to a category, pick the person, done.
  • Add a tag — added to any that haven't already got it, nothing else touched. Tags are searchable, so this is how you mark a batch you want to find again later.
  • Set status — including Archived, for a batch you want to keep but not be nagged about.

Every one of them asks first, naming the number and saying plainly what it replaces — and if a search is also narrowing the list, the question says so, because "all 12" should never be a surprise.

Undoing a bulk change

Every bulk change can be undone, and the offer does not expire when you look away.

  • Straight away: tap Undo in the bar that appears at the bottom of the screen.
  • Later — hours or days later: Settings → Data Safety. If a bulk change can still be undone, a card sits at the top of that screen naming it and when you made it: "Anna set as owner on 57 · 2 days ago". It survives closing the app, and it survives restarting the phone.

It puts back only what that change touched. Undoing an owner change three days later restores the owner and nothing else — the steps you rewrote yesterday, the photo you added this morning, the times you marked things Done all stay exactly as they are. And anything that already carried the value you applied is left as it was, rather than being blanked.

Only the most recent bulk change is held. Make a second one and it replaces the first in the queue, so if you are assigning owners category by category, undo reaches back one category, not all six. Using the undo consumes it — a change can be undone once. An instruction you have deleted since is skipped rather than resurrected, and the app tells you if that happened.

Bulk changes deliberately write nothing to Revision History: two hundred "Updated" entries would bury the real edits under bookkeeping. The safety copy on the phone is refreshed straight afterwards, as with every other change to your data — and the undo itself is kept outside your backup files on purpose, so restoring a backup never brings a half-finished change back with it.

For example: the starter library arrives with 205 instructions and no owners. Filter to Diving, ⚡ Apply to all → Anna. Filter to Cycling, ⚡ Apply to all → Martin. Six taps a category, and the person filter is suddenly worth having.

The button only ever appears while a filter is on. Acting on a whole unfiltered library from a button beside the search box is not something anybody means to do.

Related instructions

If an instruction lists related numbers, they appear at the bottom with their real titles, and tapping one takes you straight there. Rename an instruction and it updates itself everywhere it's referenced. If a related number has been deleted, the row says "No longer exists" rather than looking like a link that does nothing.

Following an instruction

Scanning a number

Tap the pink scanner on the Home tab, point the box at the printed number and hold steady, or tap "📸 Capture & Scan" for a deliberate one-shot read. Good, even lighting helps a lot.

Typing the number

Can't scan? Type the 3 digits into the number field instead. Always fast, always reliable, and it works with no camera and no light.

Keeping your place in a job

Tick the steps as you go and they stay ticked. Leave the instruction, lock the phone, come back tomorrow — you pick up where you left off. A "3 of 12 done · Reset" line appears above the steps once you have started, and Reset clears them if you want to begin again. Marking the instruction Done clears them for you, so next time you open it the list is clean.

These ticks live only on this phone and are not part of your backup — they are where you are right now, not something worth keeping.

Photos beside the step they belong to

Each photo in the editor has a "Show at step" picker. Leave it on "In the photo gallery" and the photo behaves as it always has; choose a step and the picture appears directly under that step while you're following the instruction — which is where you actually want to see it. The picker names each step by its own words, and keeps up with steps you are still typing. If you later shorten the steps, a photo left pointing at one that no longer exists quietly returns to the gallery rather than disappearing.

Marking it Done

The Done button at the bottom of an instruction records that you did it, right now. That is what starts and restarts the clock behind the Due list, and it clears your step ticks ready for next time. It does not write a revision entry — doing a job is not the same as changing the instruction.

Sharing an instruction

Open it and tap 📤 Share at the bottom. The whole instruction goes into the normal iPhone share sheet as plain text — number, title, category, frequency, time, the safety warning, equipment, what to do beforehand, the numbered steps, afterwards and notes — so you can send it by Messages or Mail, or drop it into Notes. Whoever receives it does not need the app.

Owner, audit history and revision numbers are deliberately left out: they matter in here, and mean nothing to the person you sent it to.

For example: a friend borrows the RV for a weekend. Share 100 (Pre-Drive Vehicle Check), 118 (Gas Bottle) and 140 (Emptying the Waste Tank) into one Messages thread and they have what they need, with no app to install.

Staying on top of the work

What's due

A green "Due now" card appears on the Home screen whenever something has come round again — tap it for the full list, most overdue first. This is worked out from two things the instruction already carries: its Frequency and when you last marked it Done.

So the clock only starts once you have marked an instruction Done for the first time; before that it shows "After 1st Done" and stays off the list. That is deliberate, and it is why importing the starter library doesn't fill your Home screen with two hundred overdue items. Frequencies that describe an event rather than a date — "Before each trip", "Every session", "As-needed" — never fall due, and neither does anything Archived.

For example: "Check Tyre Pressures" is Monthly. You mark it Done on the 1st of May; on the 31st it reappears on the Due list. Until that first Done, it simply waits.

Running a set of instructions

Settings → ▶️ Run a Set works through several instructions as one checklist — pick "Before each trip", everything currently due, your Favourites, or any category. Tick each one off as you go, or tap it to open the instruction in full; ← Back returns you to the run rather than to the Home screen.

A run survives you closing the app: a pink card at the top of Home shows how far through you are and takes you back to it. Finish marks everything you ticked as Done in one go, which resets the clock on all of them at once — so a departure run keeps the Due list honest without visiting eight instructions one at a time. The list is fixed when the run starts, so running "Everything due now" doesn't shrink underneath you as you tick things off.

Actions — the to-do list

Anything that needs doing goes on the Actions list: the 🗒️ Actions tab at the bottom, or the red "To-dos to tackle" card that appears on Home whenever something is open. The red number on the tab counts what is still open, so an unfinished job stays visible from wherever you are.

Tap ＋ in the top right to add one. Each to-do can carry a priority (Normal or High), an optional due date, and a link to an instruction — or none at all, for a general to-do. Open ones are listed first (High before Normal, soonest due first); tap the box to tick one off and it drops into the "✓ Done" group at the bottom, where you can tick it back open if you were too hasty.

For example: the awning arm sticks. Add a to-do "Service the awning arm", mark it High, link it to instruction 131, and it shows on the Home screen and against that instruction until it's done.

Turning an audit finding into a to-do

In an instruction's Audit Log, tap "→ Convert to Action" on any entry. The finding's first line becomes the to-do's title, the full text goes into its notes, and it stays linked to that instruction — so you can jump back to it from the Actions list by tapping the #number. Once converted, the audit entry shows "✓ Already actioned" instead of the button, so the same finding can't quietly become two separate to-dos. Backing out without saving leaves the audit entry untouched, ready to convert later.

Keeping the library honest

People

Add names once under Settings → Manage People, then pick them as Owner or "Revised by" on any instruction instead of retyping — their phone and email show up automatically in Revision History. Anna and Martin are seeded for you the first time; delete them if they're not your people.

Owner — whose job it is

Set on any instruction while editing. It is the field behind the person filter and behind "No owner" in Library Health, so filling it in is what turns a pile of instructions into two shorter lists of somebody's work.

Status

Left on Auto, the app works it out from how complete the instruction is: Draft if it hasn't got a title, description and steps; Active if it has those plus frequency, time estimate, owner and a warning; Review Needed in between. Set it by hand if you'd rather, and use Archived for anything you want to keep but not be nagged about — archived instructions never fall due.

Revision History

Open an instruction and scroll to the bottom, then tap "📋 Revision History" to expand it. It lists every saved change with the version number, date, and who made it. You choose that person from the "Revised by" dropdown while editing, and the entry appears here after you save. Marking an instruction Done and adding an audit do not create entries — this section is only for changes to the instruction itself.

Audit Log

Open any instruction, tap "🔍 Audit Log" to expand it, then record who checked it, when, and what they found. The dot next to the heading tells you at a glance whether an instruction has been audited. If the person who did the audit isn't in the "Audited by" list, tap "+ Person" beside it to add them on the spot — you'll come back to the same instruction with them selected.

Library Health

Settings → 🩺 Library Health is about the library itself rather than the RV. It shows what is overdue, what has a frequency but has never once been marked Done, what has nobody named as owner, what is still Draft or Review Needed, and which Safety or Maintenance instructions carry no warning text. Everything listed is tappable, so you can work through it.

None of it is an error — not every job needs a warning and not everything needs an owner. It's a second look, not a telling-off.

Two of the groups can fix themselves: "No owner" lets you pick a person and apply them to everything that hasn't got one, and "Safety work with no warning" can drop a holding note onto all of them at once. Both leave anything already filled in alone, so you can run them again after adding new instructions. The holding warning is honest about being a placeholder and asks to be replaced with the real hazard — treat it as a reminder, not a job finished.

Health or filters? Library Health is the report — a fixed set of questions, answered in one place. Its own fixes are deliberately blunt: they fill blanks across the whole library in one tap, and never overwrite anything already filled in. The filters are the same information while you browse, mixable with anything else, and ⚡ Apply to all acts on precisely the slice in front of you and will happily replace what's there — with an Undo behind it.

So: Health when one answer is right for everything unclaimed. Filters when the answer is different for the diving kit than it is for the RV.

Photos

An instruction can hold up to 5 photos — add them while editing, under 📷 Photos. The first one shows up as a small thumbnail beside that instruction everywhere it's listed (the Instructions tab, Favourites, Recently Viewed), which is often the fastest way to recognise the right one. Instructions with no photo simply don't show a thumbnail.

Photos are shrunk automatically when you add them — the editor shows the stored size next to each one, and what it came in at. They are still the biggest thing in your data, so if you ever add a great many, watch for the red "Safety copy on this phone has stopped" card on the Home screen and back up to Files.

Worth photographing: anything you'd otherwise describe in a paragraph — which way a valve points when it's correct, where the fuse actually sits, what the tyre wear looked like last spring so you can compare it to this spring.

Your data

Backing up

Settings → 💾 Back Up Now hands the backup straight to the normal iPhone share sheet, so you can Save to Files or put it in iCloud Drive. A 💾 card appears on the Home screen if it's been more than a week since your last one. To put a backup back, use Settings → Restore from a Backup File.

Data Safety

Settings → Data Safety shows every copy of your data the app can see — what's in the app right now, plus two automatic backups kept on this phone (a recent one and an older one), each with its date and how much it holds. If anything ever goes missing, you can put back either backup with one tap. It's also where a "Clear All Data" can be undone.

It is also where the last bulk change waits. If you have used ⚡ Apply to all and want it back, the card at the top of this screen undoes precisely that change — which is a much finer instrument than restoring a backup. The older automatic backup is roughly a day behind by design, so restoring it would also throw away everything else you have done since; the undo card throws away nothing but the change itself.

One phone, one copy

The app keeps its data on the device it's running on — the Home Screen app and Safari each have their own separate copy and do not share. Treat the Home Screen app on your phone as the real one, and use backups rather than expecting the two to match.

Offline

Your instructions are stored locally and everything works offline, including typing a number in by hand. Camera scanning needs internet the very first time to download its recognition engine (a few MB) — after that it works normally.

Dark mode

The app follows the phone and looks right either way; dark is the default, which is what you want at the bedside at night.

Five things this is actually for

1. Leaving on a trip

Settings → ▶️ Run a Set → "Before each trip". Work down the checklist, tapping into any instruction you're unsure about, then Finish. Everything you ticked is marked Done in one go and the Due list is honest again. If you get interrupted, the pink card on Home brings you back to exactly where you stopped.

2. Handing work to somebody else

Instructions tab → Filter → 👤 their name. That is their list, and nothing else. Add ⏰ Overdue if you only want to talk about what's slipped.

If a name has nothing against it yet, that's the job to do first — and it takes minutes rather than an evening. Filter to one category at a time and use ⚡ Apply to all → Set owner. Where a single person really does own everything unclaimed, Settings → 🩺 Library Health → "No owner" does the lot in one tap instead.

3. Something's wrong and you're standing in front of it

Scan the number, scroll to 🔍 Audit Log, record what you found and who found it. Then tap → Convert to Action on that entry: the finding becomes a to-do, linked to this instruction, counted on the red Actions tab until somebody deals with it. You've written it down once and it can't now be forgotten.

4. Tidying up an imported library

Two hundred instructions arrived at once and none of them know whose they are. Filter → ❓ Nobody named to see the scale of it, ⚠️ No warning for the ones missing a hazard, 🚫 No photo for the ones that would be clearer with a picture.

The ownership half is now a ten-minute job rather than an evening: filter to a category, ⚡ Apply to all → Set owner, next category. The warnings and photos still want you one at a time, because only you know the actual hazard — but do a handful each time you're sitting down anyway and the library fills itself in. ⚡ Apply to all → Add a tag is worth knowing here too: tag a batch needs-photo as you go and you can search your way straight back to them.

5. Somewhere with no signal

At the dive site, on a pitch with no bars, in an underground car park — it all still works. The instructions, the photos, the steps you tick, the to-dos you add, the Done you record. Only the very first use of the camera scanner ever needs the internet, and typing the number never does.

How the numbers are organised

001–099 is yours. Nothing else uses those numbers, so your own instructions can live there without ever colliding with anything.

The starter library uses 100 upwards, grouped by hundreds so a printed number card tells you which world it belongs to before you even scan it:

  • 100–149 — RV: driving, water, gas and power, pitch set-up, living aboard, maintenance and safety
  • 150–169 — Home: the apartment, cleaning, appliances, and leaving and returning
  • 200–224 — Diving: kit checks, buddy checks, descent and ascent, drills and care
  • 300–319 — Running: warm-ups, easy runs, intervals, tempo, long runs, cool-downs
  • 400–419 — Cycling: the M-check, sessions from Zone 2 to VO2, and bike maintenance
  • 500–519 — Swimming: warm-ups, drills, threshold sets, open water and kit care
  • 600–619 — Golf: equipment care, practice drills, and the rules worth knowing on the course
  • 700–709 — Meditation: from a three-minute reset to a full body scan
  • 800–809 — Mobility: morning flows, hips, shoulders, and post-run stretching
  • 900–909 — Strength: full-body sessions, splits, and a bodyweight one for the RV

Every one of them is yours to edit. Open it, tap Edit, and change anything — they are ordinary instructions, not locked templates.

v20.0 (2026-08-16) - YOUR DATA STOPS DISAPPEARING

What was wrong: The app kept a single safety copy of your data, and that copy could destroy itself. On starting up, the app adds Anna and Martin to the People list if it finds none — and every change immediately re-saves the safety copy as a snapshot of whatever is in the app at that moment. If the app started up empty, that first write saved an empty snapshot over the good safety copy, a split second before the rescue code went looking for it. The rescue then found nothing. That is how the instructions were lost.

What's Fixed:

  • 🛡️ A safety copy holding instructions is never replaced by an empty one. An empty app means something went wrong — not that your data is gone
  • ⏪ The rescue now runs first, before anything is allowed to write, so it can't be beaten to the punch any more
  • 👯 Two safety copies are kept now — a recent one and one at least a day older — so a single bad copy is never all that's left
  • 🔧 A second "auto-backup and recover" safety net turned out to have never run once, on any version: it was triggered by a function nothing ever called, so its backup was never written and its recovery had nothing to read. Both now use the real backups
  • 🩺 A new Data Safety screen (in Settings) shows every copy the app can see, with dates and amounts, and restores any of them with one tap
  • ↩️ "Clear All Data" can now be undone — the copy from just before it is kept in the older slot

Backing up is properly usable now: "💾 Back Up Now" hands the file to the normal iPhone share sheet, so it goes to Files or iCloud in one tap. The old version opened a page of raw text to save by hand, which in the Home Screen app could be blocked or throw you out into Safari. A 💾 reminder card appears on the Home screen if it's been more than a week.

Also honest about this: the Home Screen app and Safari keep completely separate data and never shared it. The app no longer pretends otherwise — the Home Screen app on your phone is the real one, and backups are how you keep it safe.
