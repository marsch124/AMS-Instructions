How This Works

What is AMS Instructions?

A quick-reference guide system using 3-digit numbers. Attach labeled cards with numbers (001-999) to key locations in your RV (bed, steering wheel, water systems) and scan or enter the number to see step-by-step instructions.

Getting Around

Four tabs along the bottom of the screen, always one tap away — and each one has its own colour. The whole screen takes that colour, so you can tell where you are out of the corner of your eye, before you have read a single word:

  • Home — pink. The big Scan button, your Favourites and Recently Viewed, plus any nudge cards (something due, to-dos waiting, backup overdue).
  • Instructions — green. Every instruction you have, searchable. Tap ＋ in the top right to write a new one.
  • Actions — red. Your to-do list. A red number on the tab tells you how many are still open, so you can see it without going there.
  • Settings — blue. The housekeeping: People, backups and Data Safety, this guide, and the Version Log.

Anything you open from a tab — an instruction, an editor, the People list — hides the tabs and gives you a ← Back button instead, so you always know whether you are on a main screen or inside something. It keeps the colour of the tab you came from, so a green instruction screen is one you reached from Instructions and Back will take you there.

Two colours mean the same thing everywhere, whatever tab you are on: a filled button is the thing you came to do (Save, Back Up Now), and an outlined red button is a destructive one (Delete, Clear All Data). If a button is outlined in red, read it twice.

Getting Started

  1. Create instructions: Open the 📋 Instructions tab and tap ＋ in the top right. Assign a 3-digit number (001-999) to each task. Fill in title, steps, warnings, and any other details you need.
  2. Print the numbers: Create cards or labels with 3-digit numbers (001-999). You can print just the digits, or use a free QR generator (qr-server.com) to embed the number for backup scanning.
  3. Attach to locations: Use Duro tape or adhesive to attach number cards to the relevant locations in your RV (bed, steering wheel, water tank, etc).
  4. Scan & follow: Tap the pink scanner icon (🔢 crosshair) on the Home tab, or browse the 📋 Instructions tab, enter the 3-digit number, and follow the step-by-step guide.
  5. Backup your data: Go to Settings → 💾 Back Up Now to create a backup. On iPhone, tap Share → Save to Files or Notes to keep your instructions safe.

Tips

  • Share instructions: When viewing an instruction, tap the "📱 Share via SMS" button at the bottom to send a completion message via text with the task name and location.
  • What's due: A green "Due now" card appears on the Home screen whenever something has come round again — tap it for the full list, most overdue first. This is worked out from two things the instruction already carries: its Frequency and when you last marked it Done. So the clock only starts once you have marked an instruction Done for the first time; before that it shows "After 1st Done" and stays off the list. That is deliberate, and it is why importing the starter library doesn't fill your Home screen with two hundred overdue items. Frequencies that describe an event rather than a date — "Before each trip", "Every session", "As-needed" — never fall due, and neither does anything Archived.
  • Keeping your place in a job: Tick the steps as you go and they stay ticked. Leave the instruction, lock the phone, come back tomorrow — you pick up where you left off. A "3 of 12 done · Reset" line appears above the steps once you have started, and Reset clears them if you want to begin again. Marking the instruction Done clears them for you, so the next time you open it the list is clean. These ticks live only on this phone and are not part of your backup — they are where you are right now, not something worth keeping.
  • Favorites: Tap ★ on any instruction to save it for quick access from the home screen.
  • People: Add names once under Settings → Manage People, then pick them as Owner or "Revised by" on any instruction instead of retyping — their phone/email shows up automatically in Revision History.
  • Version Log: What changed in each release of the app lives on its own screen — Settings → Version Log. This guide is about how to use the app; the Version Log is about what's new.
  • Revision History: Open an instruction and scroll to the bottom, then tap "📋 Revision History" to expand it. It lists every saved change with the version number, date, and who made it. You choose that person from the "Revised by" dropdown while editing, and the entry appears here after you save. Marking an instruction Done and adding an audit do not create entries — this section is only for changes to the instruction itself.
  • Audit Log: Open any instruction, tap "🔍 Audit Log" to expand it, then record who checked it, when, and what they found. The dot next to the heading tells you at a glance whether an instruction has been audited. If the person who did the audit isn't in the "Audited by" list, tap "+ Person" beside it to add them on the spot — you'll come back to the same instruction with them selected.
  • Actions (to-dos): Anything that needs doing goes on the Actions list — the 🗒️ Actions tab at the bottom, or the red "🗒️ To-dos to tackle" card that appears on the Home screen whenever something is open. The red number on the Actions tab counts what is still open, so an unfinished job stays visible from wherever you are. Tap ＋ in the top right to add one. Each to-do can carry a priority (Normal or High), an optional due date, and a link to an instruction — or none at all, for a general to-do. Open ones are listed first (High before Normal, soonest due first); tap the box to tick one off and it drops into the "✓ Done" group at the bottom, where you can tick it back open if you were too hasty.
  • Turning an audit finding into a to-do: In an instruction's Audit Log, tap "→ Convert to Action" on any entry. The finding's first line becomes the to-do's title, the full text goes into its notes, and it stays linked to that instruction — so you can jump back to it from the Actions list by tapping the #number. Once converted, the audit entry shows "✓ Already actioned" instead of the button, so the same finding can't quietly become two separate to-dos. Backing out without saving leaves the audit entry untouched, ready to convert later.
  • Scanning a number: Point the box at the printed number and hold steady, or tap "📸 Capture & Scan" for a deliberate one-shot read. Good, even lighting helps a lot.
  • Manual entry: Can't scan the number card? Just enter the 3-digit number manually in the number input field — always fast and 100% reliable.
  • Backing up: Settings → 💾 Back Up Now hands the backup straight to the normal iPhone share sheet, so you can Save to Files or put it in iCloud Drive. A 💾 card appears on the Home screen if it's been more than a week since your last one. To put a backup back, use Settings → Restore from a Backup File.
  • Data Safety: Settings → Data Safety shows every copy of your data the app can see — what's in the app right now, plus two automatic backups kept on this phone (a recent one and an older one), each with its date and how much it holds. If anything ever goes missing, you can put back either backup with one tap. It's also where a "Clear All Data" can be undone.
  • One phone, one copy: The app keeps its data on the device it's running on — the Home Screen app and Safari each have their own separate copy and do not share. Treat the Home Screen app on your phone as the real one, and use backups rather than expecting the two to match.
  • Offline: Your instructions are stored locally and everything works offline, including manual number entry. Camera scanning needs internet the very first time to download its recognition engine (a few MB) — after that it works normally.
  • Dark mode: The app defaults to dark mode, perfect for bedside use at night. Custom pink accents help tasks pop even in low light.
  • Browsing by category: The 📋 Instructions tab is grouped into folding sections — one per category, each with a coloured dot and a count. Tap a header to open it, tap again to close. It remembers what you left open, and "Expand all" / "Collapse all" beside the count line does the lot at once. This is the way to find something when you do not know what to search for.
  • Photos: An instruction can hold up to 5 photos — add them while editing, under 📷 Photos. The first one shows up as a small thumbnail beside that instruction everywhere it's listed (the Instructions tab, Favourites, Recently Viewed), which is often the fastest way to recognise the right one. Instructions with no photo simply don't show a thumbnail. Photos are shrunk automatically when you add them — the editor shows the stored size next to each one, and what it came in at. They are still the biggest thing in your data, so if you ever add a great many, watch for the red "Safety copy on this phone has stopped" card on the Home screen and back up to Files.
  • Search: Type in the search box and the groups drop away, giving you a plain list of every match across all categories, with the category shown on each result. Searching by number works too. The box clears itself each time you come back to the tab, so you never land on a filtered list wondering where everything went.
  • Search looks inside everything: not just titles, but steps, description, warnings, equipment, preparations, after-use, maintenance, notes, location and owner. So a tyre pressure you once typed into step 4 is findable by typing the number, and searching a person's name brings back everything they own. Where the match is somewhere you cannot see from the list, the result says so underneath — "matched in Steps" — so a hit on hidden text never looks like a mistake.
  • Related instructions: If an instruction lists related numbers, they appear at the bottom with their real titles, and tapping one takes you straight there. Rename an instruction and it updates itself everywhere it's referenced. If a related number has been deleted, the row says "No longer exists" rather than looking like a link that does nothing.

How the Numbers Are Organised

001–099 is yours. Nothing else uses those numbers, so your own instructions can live there without ever colliding with anything.

The starter library uses 100 upwards, grouped by hundreds so a printed number card tells you which world it belongs to before you even scan it:

  • 100–149 — RV: driving, water, gas and power, pitch set-up, living aboard, maintenance and safety
  • 150–169 — Home: the apartment, cleaning, appliances, and leaving and returning
  • 200–224 — Diving: kit checks, buddy checks, descent and ascent, drills and care
  • 300–319 — Running: warm-ups, easy runs, intervals, tempo, long runs, cool-downs
  • 400–419 — Cycling: the M-check, sessions from Zone 2 to VO2, and bike maintenance
  • 500–519 — Swimming: warm-ups, drills, threshold sets, open water and kit care
  • 600–619 — Golf: equipment care, practice drills, and the rules worth knowing on the course
  • 700–709 — Meditation: from a three-minute reset to a full body scan
  • 800–809 — Mobility: morning flows, hips, shoulders, and post-run stretching
  • 900–909 — Strength: full-body sessions, splits, and a bodyweight one for the RV

Every one of them is yours to edit. Open it, tap Edit, and change anything — they are ordinary instructions, not locked templates.

v20.0 (2026-08-16) - YOUR DATA STOPS DISAPPEARING

What was wrong: The app kept a single safety copy of your data, and that copy could destroy itself. On starting up, the app adds Anna and Martin to the People list if it finds none — and every change immediately re-saves the safety copy as a snapshot of whatever is in the app at that moment. If the app started up empty, that first write saved an empty snapshot over the good safety copy, a split second before the rescue code went looking for it. The rescue then found nothing. That is how the instructions were lost.

What's Fixed:

  • 🛡️ A safety copy holding instructions is never replaced by an empty one. An empty app means something went wrong — not that your data is gone
  • ⏪ The rescue now runs first, before anything is allowed to write, so it can't be beaten to the punch any more
  • 👯 Two safety copies are kept now — a recent one and one at least a day older — so a single bad copy is never all that's left
  • 🔧 A second "auto-backup and recover" safety net turned out to have never run once, on any version: it was triggered by a function nothing ever called, so its backup was never written and its recovery had nothing to read. Both now use the real backups
  • 🩺 A new Data Safety screen (in Settings) shows every copy the app can see, with dates and amounts, and restores any of them with one tap
  • ↩️ "Clear All Data" can now be undone — the copy from just before it is kept in the older slot

Backing up is properly usable now: "💾 Back Up Now" hands the file to the normal iPhone share sheet, so it goes to Files or iCloud in one tap. The old version opened a page of raw text to save by hand, which in the Home Screen app could be blocked or throw you out into Safari. A 💾 reminder card appears on the Home screen if it's been more than a week.

Also honest about this: the Home Screen app and Safari keep completely separate data and never shared it. The app no longer pretends otherwise — the Home Screen app on your phone is the real one, and backups are how you keep it safe.

v14.0 (2026-08-13) - FIXED THE AUTO VERSION-SYNC ITSELF

Fixed: The background check that's supposed to notice when Safari and the Home Screen app fall out of sync and automatically fix it had never actually been running, on any version — Safari staying ahead of Home Screen after an update relied entirely on manually force-closing the Home Screen app.

Cause: That checker read the app's version number one script file too early, before that file had loaded — which silently crashed it before it ever started watching.

What changed:
